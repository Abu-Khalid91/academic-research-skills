\textbf{Key Points}\\
\textbf{Question}\enspace Can a single bounded score tell when a recursive multi-agent large language model (LLM)
consensus in the intensive care unit is too poorly grounded in fresh physiological data to be shown to clinicians?\\
\textbf{Findings}\enspace In this methods and simulation study, the proposed AlSamadani Score ($\Sal\in[0,100]$)
combines temporal data sparsity, collective epistemic drift, dissent collapse, and a sparsity-dependent confidence
ceiling. On 2 author-constructed vignettes, it graded a dense-data encounter Tier~I ($\Sal=\CaseAScore$) and
blocked the consensus for a sparse admission-triage encounter (Tier~III, $\Sal=\CaseBScore$), for which arterial
lactate had the largest counterfactual value of information (+\TopVOIPoints{} points). In \MCN{} synthetic encounters, the
score discriminated correct from incorrect consensus with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of
\MCAUROC{} (95\% CI, \MCAUROCLo--\MCAUROCHi), versus \MCAUROCConf{} for raw consensus confidence.\\
\textbf{Meaning}\enspace These findings justify validating the score on real data with a prespecified protocol before any use
at the bedside. They are not evidence of clinical benefit.}}

\begin{abstract}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Importance}\enspace Multi-agent LLM systems are being proposed for critical care. Iterative debate can
make agents look more consistent and more confident without adding any evidence. At intensive care unit (ICU) admission,
when laboratory data are sparse or stale, that pattern risks anchoring and automation bias.\\
\textbf{Objective}\enspace To specify and test the internal behavior of a fail-closed governance score that decides
when a multi-agent consensus may be displayed, should be displayed only as differentials, or must be withheld.\\
\textbf{Design, Setting, and Participants}\enspace A methods study with deterministic vignettes and a seeded Monte Carlo
simulation ($N=\MCN$ encounters across 4 sparsity and confidence-laundering scenarios), plus a scaling benchmark
($M\in\{3,5,7,10\}$ agents, $R\in\{1,2,4,6\}$ rounds) with and without a ``catfish'' dissenting agent.
No patient data were used. Open-source code includes a loader for MIMIC-IV v3.1 and eICU-CRD for future validation.\\
\textbf{Main Outcomes and Measures}\enspace Governance tier, AUROC of $\Sal$ for correct consensus, expected calibration error (ECE)
before and after the confidence ceiling, and dissent-collapse ratio.\\
\textbf{Results}\enspace The dense-data vignette was graded Tier~I ($\Sal=\CaseAScore$) and the sparse admission vignette
Tier~III ($\Sal=\CaseBScore$; consensus withheld; deterministic sepsis-bundle fallback issued). In simulation, the AUROC was
\MCAUROC{} (95\% CI, \MCAUROCLo--\MCAUROCHi) versus \MCAUROCConf{} for raw confidence. Consensus accuracy was
\MCTierOneAcc\% in Tier~I and \MCTierThreeAcc\% in Tier~III (4-class problem; chance, 25\%). Capping confidence lowered
ECE from \MCECERaw{} to \MCECECapped. The catfish agent reduced dissent collapse and raised $\Sal$ but did
not improve consensus accuracy. The dissent component can therefore be inflated without any epistemic gain.\\
\textbf{Conclusions and Relevance}\enspace The AlSamadani Score is a transparent, fail-closed rule for withholding
under-grounded LLM consensus. Its weights are expert priors, and its performance figures are synthetic.
Prospective, TRIPOD+AI--guided validation is required before clinical use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLM agents are increasingly evaluated on ICU data. AgentRx benchmarked LLM agents on multimodal clinical risk
prediction using MIMIC ICU data. In that study, single-agent configurations outperformed naive multi-agent baselines,
and both trailed supervised fusion models.\cite{agentrx} Agentic systems now run complete emergency department workflows
on MIMIC-IV cases.\cite{mira} Debate among agents has well-known benefits for factuality on benchmark tasks.\cite{du2024}
In medical question answering, however, Wang and Yang showed that debate can produce a \emph{consistency illusion}.
Agents stop contradicting each other while the similarity of their reasoning chains falls, as measured by their
cross-agent reasoning alignment (CARA) metrics.\cite{wangyang}
In a 3-agent retrieval-augmented system for early sepsis management evaluated by 2 intensivists,
hallucinated clinical findings appeared in 3 of 10 cases.\cite{iapascurta}

These observations matter most at ICU admission. At that point lactate, arterial blood gases, and coagulation
studies are often pending or hours old. A confident consensus formed then may rest on population priors rather than
patient data. Known failure modes of clinical decision support compound the risk. The most relevant are automation bias\cite{goddard} and
inflated or poorly calibrated outputs from widely deployed sepsis models.\cite{wong2021} Calibration metrics such as ECE\cite{guo2017} assess a model's
confidence but say nothing about whether the evidence for that confidence is present at the moment it is displayed.

We propose the AlSamadani Score ($\Sal$), a bounded, fully specified governance layer that sits
\emph{between} a multi-agent system and the clinician. It answers 1 operational question: whether the consensus should be
shown, shown only as differentials, or withheld. It also says which data to obtain. The score is \emph{fail-closed}:
any missing input, invalid trace, or software error resolves to abstention. Abstention is never empty: a deterministic
guideline engine evaluated on observed data supplies a bedside fallback.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Setting and notation}
An encounter $e$ is evaluated at time $t$ (hours since ICU admission). $M$ specialist agents
deliberate for $R$ rounds over $K$ candidate diagnoses. Agent $m$ in round $r$ emits a probability vector
$p_{m,r}\in\Delta^{K-1}$; round 0 holds the independent first opinions. The consensus is
$\bar p_r=\tfrac1M\sum_m p_{m,r}$ and its confidence is $c_r=\max_k \bar p_{r,k}$. All divergences use the base-2
Jensen--Shannon divergence $\JSD\in[0,1]$.\cite{lin1991}

\subsection{Temporal-Physiological Sparsity Index}
Eight canonical sepsis and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) biomarkers\cite{singer2016,vincent1996} carry
decision weights $w_j$ ($\sum_j w_j=1$) and decision-relevance half-lives $h_j$ (Table~1). If $m_j\in\{0,1\}$ indicates
that biomarker $j$ has been observed and $\Delta t_j$ is its elapsed age in hours, then
\begin{equation}
\sigma(x_t)=1-\sum_{j=1}^{8} w_j\, m_j\, e^{-\lambda_j \Delta t_j},\qquad \lambda_j=\frac{\ln 2}{h_j},\qquad \sigma\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
The index is 0 when every biomarker is observed at $\Delta t_j=0$ and 1 when none is available. The half-lives encode how
fast each value loses decision relevance in shock, not its pharmacokinetics. The weights and half-lives are expert
priors set by the author before any simulation. They were not fitted to data.

\subsection{Collective Epistemic Drift}
Drift is the cumulative change in the consensus across rounds plus a penalty for \emph{confidence laundering}, ie,
confidence that rises faster than grounded evidence:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CED}_R=\min\Bigl\{1,\;\sum_{r=1}^{R}\JSD(\bar p_{r-1},\bar p_r)\;+\;\gamma\,\max\bigl(0,\;\Delta c-\Delta I_{\mathrm{evid}}\bigr)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta c=c_R-c_0$ and $\Delta I_{\mathrm{evid}}=I_R-I_0$, with $I_r$ the grounded-evidence information available
at round $r$ (eg, $1-\sigma$ if new results arrive during deliberation; otherwise constant, so $\Delta I_{\mathrm{evid}}=0$).
We set $\gamma=1$.

\subsection{Dissent Collapse Ratio under sparsity}
With mean pairwise disagreement $D_r=\binom{M}{2}^{-1}\sum_{m<m'}\JSD(p_{m,r},p_{m',r})$,
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}=\max\bigl(0,\,1-D_R/D_0\bigr)\quad(\rho=0\text{ if }D_0<10^{-3}).
\end{equation}
Agents converging on dense data is expected and benign. Convergence on sparse data is the warning sign. The score
therefore uses the sparsity-weighted product $\sigma\rho$.

\subsection{Sparsity-Penalized Calibration Error and the Epistemic Confidence Ceiling}
The confidence the available data can support is capped at
\begin{equation}
c_{\max}(\sigma)=1-\kappa\sigma,\qquad
\mathrm{SPCE}_e=\frac{\max\bigl(0,\,c_R-c_{\max}\bigr)}{\kappa}\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
with $\kappa=0.5$. Thus a fully sparse encounter cannot display a probability above 0.5. At cohort level,
$\mathrm{SPCE}=\mathrm{ECE}\bigl(\min(c_i,c_{\max,i})\bigr)+\overline{\mathrm{SPCE}_e}$, combining the residual
miscalibration of capped confidences\cite{guo2017,naeini2015} with the mean excess over the ceiling.

\subsection{The AlSamadani Score}
\begin{equation}
\Sal=100\Bigl[1-\bigl(\alpha_\sigma\,\sigma+\alpha_D\,\mathrm{CED}_R+\alpha_\rho\,\sigma\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}+\alpha_C\,\mathrm{SPCE}_e\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
with prespecified weights $(\alpha_\sigma,\alpha_D,\alpha_\rho,\alpha_C)=(0.40,0.20,0.20,0.20)$.
\textit{Boundedness.} Each component lies in $[0,1]$ and the $\alpha$ weights sum to 1, so $\Sal\in[0,100]$.
\textit{Monotonicity.} For a fixed deliberation trace,
$\partial\Sal/\partial\sigma=-100\bigl(\alpha_\sigma+\alpha_\rho\rho+\alpha_C\,\mathbb 1[c_R>c_{\max}]\bigr)\le0$
almost everywhere. Fresher or more complete data never lower the score.

\subsection{Counterfactual value of information}
For each biomarker, $\mathrm{VOI}_j=\Sal(x_t\mid \Delta t_j{=}0,\,m_j{=}1)-\Sal(x_t)$. The deliberation trace is held fixed,
so VOI measures how much of the score the governance layer would recover if that test were drawn now; it does not
predict how the agents would update. By Eq.~5, while the ceiling stays active,
$\mathrm{VOI}_j=100\,w_j(1-m_j e^{-\lambda_j\Delta t_j})(\alpha_\sigma+\alpha_\rho\rho+\alpha_C)$. VOI therefore scales with
each biomarker's lost information multiplied by the encounter's collapse level. Tests are ranked by $\mathrm{VOI}_j$.

\subsection{Governance tiers and the fail-closed fallback}
Table~2 shows the tiers. In Tier~III the software sets \texttt{consensus\_diagnosis\_index = None} and returns no
probability. The aim is to prevent anchoring and to spare clinicians the work of verifying an under-grounded answer.
Validation errors, missing traces, or exceptions also resolve to Tier~III. In every tier a deterministic rule engine
evaluates the \emph{observed} vital signs and laboratory values against the 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) adult
guideline\cite{ssc2026} and the KDIGO acute kidney injury guideline.\cite{kdigo2012} The SSC rules cover antimicrobials
ideally within 1 hour when shock is possible, lactate measurement when it is unavailable, and individualized balanced
crystalloid resuscitation (30 mL/kg considered within 3 hours, reassessed with dynamic measures). They also cover
norepinephrine as first-line vasopressor with an initial mean arterial pressure (MAP) target of 65 mm Hg (60--65 mm Hg
at age 65 years or older) and lung-protective ventilation at 6 mL/kg predicted body weight. The KDIGO rules cover AKI
staging with nephrotoxin avoidance. The engine never imputes values. It returns at least 1 recommendation by construction,
which we call guideline coverage; coverage is not evidence of efficacy. Every recommendation is a prompt for clinician
review, not an order.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{De-identify}: replace patient, stay, and unit identifiers with HMAC tokens; convert timestamps to

\item \emph{Ground}: compute $\sigma(x_t)$ from the observed biomarker ages (Eq.~1).
\item \emph{Deliberate} (external multi-agent system): collect $p_{m,r}$ for $r=0,\dots,R$ and the round-0 rationales.
\item \emph{Audit}: compute $\mathrm{CED}_R$, $\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}$, $c_{\max}$, and $\mathrm{SPCE}_e$ (Eqs~2--4), then $\Sal$ (Eq.~5).
\item \emph{Decide}: assign the tier (Table~2); on any exception, assign Tier~III and set \texttt{consensus\_diagnosis\_index = None}.
\item \emph{Act}: always run the deterministic SSC 2026/KDIGO engine on observed data; rank tests by $\mathrm{VOI}_j$.
\item \emph{Log}: store the tokens, the component values, the tier, and the fallback rule identifiers for audit. Store no raw identifiers.
\end{enumerate}}}

\subsection{Privacy architecture}
Patient and unit identifiers are replaced by salted, domain-separated HMAC-SHA256 tokens of the form
\texttt{ANON-}\allowbreak\texttt{XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX} (16 hexadecimal characters). The salt is held outside the model
context. If no salt is configured, the software refuses to run (fail-closed). A regular-expression scrubber redacts
names, record numbers, Saudi national ID and Iqama numbers, telephone numbers, email addresses, calendar dates, and
facility names. All times are converted to elapsed hours ($\Delta t_j$) relative to ICU admission. A final guard
raises an error if any personal health information pattern or raw identifier survives into the prompt. These are
technical safeguards only. Lawful use requires local governance under the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL),
policies of the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA), and, for MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD, PhysioNet
credentialing and the data use agreement. The agreement restricts transmitting data to third-party online services.\cite{mimic4,eicu}

\subsection{Simulation design}
The simulation was prespecified, and all parameters were fixed before the first run (seed 20261110). In each encounter,
the true diagnosis $y$ was drawn from $K=4$ classes. Each biomarker was observed with probability
$q\in\{0.95,0.60,0.25,0.25\}$ (scenarios S0--S3), with age drawn from $\mathcal U(0,kh_j)$, $k\in\{1,2,3,3\}$.
Agent logits were $\beta e_y+b+\varepsilon_m$, where the signal was $\beta=3(1-\sigma)$, $b\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ was a bias
shared by all agents (common model family), and $\varepsilon_m\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ was agent-specific noise.
Each round applied conformity and sharpening, $p\leftarrow\mathrm{norm}\bigl[((1-\eta)p+\eta\bar p)^{1/\tau}\bigr]$, with
$\eta=0.5$. We used $\tau=0.9$ in S0--S2 and $\tau=0.6$ in S3 to model confidence laundering. The base case had $M=5$
and $R=2$. The outcome was a correct argmax consensus. We report the AUROC from the Mann-Whitney statistic with a
1000-replicate bootstrap 95\% CI. In the scaling benchmark, run under S3 conditions with 200 encounters per cell and
common random numbers across arms, the catfish agent kept its independent round-0 distribution in every round.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Vignettes}
Case A had complete laboratory data, each value 1 hour old or less, and its agents agreed early. Case B was an admission triage
at $T=0$ hours with no lactate, blood gas, coagulation, or liver tests, and with 3 laboratory values 3 hours old. Its
agents disagreed initially, then converged with rising confidence and no new evidence (Table~3). Case A scored
$\Sal=\CaseAScore$ (Tier~I). Case B scored $\Sal=\CaseBScore$ (Tier~III). For Case B the consensus was withheld, and
the fallback issued the antimicrobial, stat-lactate, fluid, MAP, and ventilation prompts. Case B's laundering term was
\CaseBLaundering: its confidence of \CaseBConf{} exceeded its ceiling of \CaseBCeiling. Arterial lactate ranked first by
VOI (+\TopVOIPoints{} points), followed by the \SecondVOILabel{} (+\SecondVOIPoints) and bicarbonate (+\ThirdVOIPoints).
These values match the closed form in Methods: $0.22\times(0.40+0.20\times\CaseBRho+0.20)\times100$ for lactate.

Across \MCN{} synthetic encounters, $\Sal$ discriminated correct from incorrect consensus with an AUROC of \MCAUROC{}

\subsection{Scaling and the catfish agent}
Without a dissenter, $\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}$ rose toward 1 as rounds increased at every panel size, and $\Sal$ fell
accordingly (Table~5). A single catfish agent kept $\rho$ substantially lower and raised mean $\Sal$ by up to about 25
points. Consensus accuracy, however, was essentially unchanged (differences of 1.0 percentage point or less in every
cell). In this generative model, preserved dissent therefore restored the \emph{score} without improving the
\emph{answer}.

\section{Discussion}
The AlSamadani Score turns 4 failure signatures of recursive multi-agent deliberation into a single, auditable display
rule: missing or stale data, drift unsupported by evidence, collapse of dissent under sparsity, and confidence above
what the data support. Its distinguishing design choice is that abstention is \emph{active}. When the AI is silenced,
the clinician receives the guideline bundle and a ranked list of the tests most likely to restore a usable assessment.
In the sparse admission vignette, arterial lactate topped that list. Lactate carries the largest weight and the
shortest half-life in the panel, and SSC 2026 places it at the center of early resuscitation.\cite{ssc2026}

The simulation supports the internal logic of the score. Scores were lower when data were sparser, and the tiers
separated consensus accuracy. The confidence ceiling also reduced calibration error. The AUROC of \MCAUROC{} is modest,
however, and it is a property of our generative model, not of any real agent system. We report it as such, without
tuning the parameters to reach a target value.

The most important finding is negative. A stubborn dissenter lowers $\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}$ by construction and so
raises $\Sal$ without improving accuracy. A naive implementation could therefore be gamed: adding a contrarian agent
would move encounters out of Tier~III with no epistemic gain. This mirrors the consistency illusion described by Wang
and Yang\cite{wangyang} in the opposite direction, where \emph{apparent} diversity is mistaken for grounded diversity.
Before validation, we recommend 3 changes: (1) require catfish dissent to cite observed data, for example by weighting
$D_r$ with a reasoning-alignment measure such as CARA;\cite{wangyang} (2) exclude designated dissenters from $D_r$; or
(3) cap the credit the dissent term can contribute.

\subsection{Limitations}
First, no patient data were analyzed. All performance figures are synthetic and depend on the assumed generative model.
Second, the weights, half-lives, $\gamma$, $\kappa$, and the tier thresholds are expert priors and have not been
calibrated. Third, VOI is first order: it holds the agents' beliefs fixed. Fourth, the panel omits the nonlaboratory
SOFA components and cardiac-specific markers that matter in the cardiovascular ICU (CVICU), such as troponin,
natriuretic peptides, and mixed venous saturation. Fifth, the regular-expression scrubber is not a certified
de-identification tool. Sixth, the guideline engine paraphrases SSC 2026 and KDIGO, and its wording and recommendation
strengths must be checked against the primary texts before deployment.

\subsection{Planned validation}
We will register a protocol for retrospective validation. It will use MIMIC-IV v3.1 for development and eICU-CRD for
external evaluation, with the bundled loader. We will report it according to TRIPOD+AI\cite{tripodai} (eTable).
Prespecified outcomes are discrimination, calibration, and net benefit of $\Sal$ for consensus error, together with the
time to and yield of VOI-ranked tests. Prospective silent-mode evaluation at the authors' institutions would follow,
subject to ethics approval.

\subsection{Regulatory considerations}
We designed the software with reference to the 4 nondevice clinical decision support criteria of FD\&C Act
\S520(o)(1)(E), as added by 21st Century Cures Act \S3060(a), and the US Food and Drug Administration's 2026 clinical
decision support guidance.\cite{fdacds} Time-critical ICU use can place software outside those criteria. We make no
regulatory determination; formal review is required. The design also draws on World Health Organization guidance on
AI for health\cite{who2024} and on Saudi PDPL and SDAIA requirements.

\section{Conclusions}
The AlSamadani Score is a transparent, fail-closed rule for withholding under-grounded multi-agent LLM consensus in the
ICU. It always provides a guideline fallback and data-acquisition guidance in its place. The score is ready for
prespecified validation. It is not ready for clinical use.

## 1. Publishers are buying citation checking from vendors rather than building it (Apr–Sep 2026)
- This answers the open follow-up "has a major publisher deployed a named reference-check tool?" Not in-house, but the market is consolidating around **Grounded AI's Veracity**. Reported integrations: SciFlow (writing), Emerald Publishing, IOP Publishing, and Silverchair's **ScholarOne Integrity Checks** (reported 2026-09-29, from one aggregator source only). Veracity flags references that are retracted, mismatched or unverifiable at submission.
- The Nature analysis with Grounded AI (Nature 652, 2 Apr 2026) covered Elsevier, Sage, Springer Nature, T&F and Wiley. Reported estimates for 2025 papers with invalid references range from "tens of thousands" to "110,000+", which is not settled. In Nature's manual check of the 100 most suspicious papers, 65 had an invalid reference. **Veracity flagged only 13 of those papers**, per secondary sources.
- Digital Science, a Holtzbrinck sibling of Springer Nature, reportedly has an in-house submission-time integrity tool that includes hallucinated-citation flags. Also reported, from a secondary source: arXiv now threatens a one-year ban when there is incontrovertible evidence that LLM output went unchecked.
- **So what:** the buyer is the submission system (ScholarOne, editorial platforms), not the individual author. If the 13/65 figure holds, the leading vendor misses a large share of fakes. That supports the ARS selling point of four-index triangulation plus a measured false-positive rate.
- **Business angle:** the route to market is an **integration partnership with a manuscript platform**, not selling to journals one by one. Silverchair already runs a partner marketplace (Integra EditorialPilot, Imagetwin, CACTUS, Hum Alchemist). No pricing was found, so I make no revenue claim.

## 2. Multi-agent debate: "collapse vs correction" is now a NeurIPS 2026 metric (Sep 2026)
- *Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate* (Li, Liu, Yuen; arXiv 2609.35279; NeurIPS 2026 poster). Final accuracy hides opposing effects. In one example GLM-4.6 panel, accuracy barely moved (76.0→75.5%), while debate collapsed 6 of 152 correct majorities and corrected 5 of 48 wrong ones. A freeze policy that prevents collapse lost 108 corrections in order to prevent 29 collapses. An 8-probe pre-debate screen tracks collapse risk (ρ = 0.893, family level). One secondary summary gives the scale as about 6.9k debates.
- Related: *The Cost of Consensus* (2605.00914) reports 98.1 mean confidence alongside 14.4% accuracy under debate, and modal sycophancy of up to 95.4%. A CAIS 2026 paper whose title is about decomposing "sycophancy fragility / consensus collapse" in homogeneous panels was seen by title only.
- None of these are clinical. The closest clinical work, SycoEval-EM (2601.16529), tests one agent being pressured by a patient agent, not agents debating each other.
- **So what:** this is the closest prior art yet for the AlSamadani Score's dissent-collapse dimension. **Adopt or cite the transition-ledger framing** (collapse, correction, onset, signed utility), and note its lesson: a collapse-only metric can recommend the wrong policy. The ICU or clinical version of this measurement still appears to be open.

## 3. Claim-to-source benchmarks are maturing, but none is a standard yet (Feb–Jun 2026)
- CiteAudit (2602.23452) is a benchmark plus a multi-agent checker for unsupported or misattributed claims. CiteCheck (2605.27700) is a 982-citation physics benchmark that grades errors as exact, minor or major, and it checks how references are specified, not claim support. AuthorityBench (2606.13104) finds that fabricated citations raise LLM acceptance of claims by 3–22 percentage points.
- **So what:** CiteAudit is the obvious external benchmark to run the ARS L3 anchor layer against before making a public precision claim.

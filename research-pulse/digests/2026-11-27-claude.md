## 1. AI-written peer reviews: conferences now allow AI with disclosure, and detection is where it gets hard (Mar–Nov 2026)
- **ICLR 2027 policy.** Reviewers may use AI if they disclose it. A review that contains LLM-generated falsehoods or hallucinations is grounds for disciplinary action. ICLR reserves the right to run AI detectors. It also asks reviewers to share the LLM inputs from every turn of the conversation.
- **ICML 2026 enforcement.** ICML hid instructions inside submission PDFs so that LLM-generated reviews would carry detectable markers. This flagged 795 reviews, about 1% of all reviews. ICML says this probably catches only the most careless cases.
- **Detector accuracy (arXiv 2603.20450).** Pangram caught 98.3% and GPTZero 95.8% of fully AI-written reviews. Both had a high false-positive rate on human reviews that had been polished by an LLM, and polishing is exactly the use most policies allow.
- **So what:** a policy that allows disclosed AI use but punishes hallucinations moves enforcement from "was AI used?" to "is the content grounded?". That is the problem the ARS anchor and verification layers already target. It also makes `academic-paper disclosure` mode more relevant on the reviewer side.

## 2. AI pre-submission review is a paid consumer market, and the price floor is being pushed down (2026)
- **Paid tools.** Reported prices:
  - Refine.ink: about $30–50 per review, depending on bundle.
  - Reviewer3: $19 per one-off review, or $29/month for unlimited reviews. Teams pricing is by quote.

  Both sets of figures come from Manusights, which competes with these tools, so treat them with caution.
- **Free and near-free tools.** Stanford's Agentic Reviewer (PaperReview.ai, from Andrew Ng's group) is free and positioned as first-pass triage. An open-source not-for-profit reviewer claims it costs less than $2 per review and matches these tools. That claim is self-reported.
- **Business angle:** the evidence shows real willingness to pay ($19–50 per review), but the free tools are squeezing generic feedback. The ARS reviewer differs in three ways: a multi-reviewer panel, a Devil's Advocate critique with scored rebuttals, and claim-to-source grounding. Lambda's "ReviewGrounder" post (seen by title only) suggests others are moving toward grounded review too. I found no funding rounds for Refine or Reviewer3.

## 3. AI-scientist systems: published in Nature, but citations are still a known weakness (Mar–Sep 2026)
- **Nature paper.** *Towards end-to-end automation of AI research* (Lu, Lu, Lange, … Clune; Nature 651:914–919, 26 Mar 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10265-5) presents Sakana's AI Scientist. Sakana lists **inaccurate citations** among the known weaknesses, and the system so far works only in computer science.
- **ALIFE 2026.** An "Agentic Scientist" pipeline co-authored a paper accepted at ALIFE 2026. How much human editing went into it was not disclosed.
- **ScientistTwo (arXiv 2609.19644).** Claims its papers beat the average scores of accepted ICLR and NeurIPS papers. Those scores came from *automated* reviewers, so AI is grading AI. A benchmarking study, *Can AI Evaluate AI Scientists?* (2607.28631), looks at that loop (seen by title only).
- **So what / business angle:** each AI-scientist pipeline needs a citation-integrity step it can trust. A standalone gate such as the ARS v3.11 `verify_passport.py` API and CLI could serve as a plug-in for these pipelines. That is an idea for a new market, not a market the evidence shows exists.

## 4. Clinical multi-agent LLMs: small studies still find hallucinations and low expert agreement (2026)
- **Moldova study.** A Technical University of Moldova group published a follow-up in Healthcare Informatics Research (vol. 32, no. 2, 2026; the month was not stated). Their system uses three agents (sepsis management, antibiotics, and guideline compliance), running PalmyraMed 70B, compared against GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4o mini. It was tested on 10 cases. Hallucinations appeared in 3 of the 10 cases, and expert agreement was low (κ = 0.26). The authors conclude it needs human oversight.
- **Nothing new since 11-23.** I found no other multi-agent ICU or sepsis study dated Nov 2026. COMPOSER-LLM is still due 2026-12-01. PAUSE-Agents has no new version.
- **So what:** this is one more feasibility study that measures neither dissent collapse nor confidence drift. That gap is what the AlSamadani Score targets, and it is still open.

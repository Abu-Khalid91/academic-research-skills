## 1. The AlSamadani Score niche is filling up fast (Oct–Nov 2026)
The 2026-11-22 scan said nobody measured collective epistemic drift or dissent collapse. That is **no longer true**. At least 15 arXiv papers from mid-October to 20 November cover it:
- **"Believed but Not Said"** (UIC, 15 Oct 2026, arXiv 2610.22138) **[read]**. In ten-agent discussions, 1 adversarial agent left 44.8% of benign agents privately holding a false answer, and 3 agents left 60.5% (the no-attacker baseline was 0.6%). The key result is **hidden infection**: with 3 attackers, agents privately held the false answer in 51.6% of turns but publicly endorsed it in only 19.7%. Monitoring the transcript misses about two-thirds of the damage. Agents that knew the truth publicly rejected the false claim in only 61.9% of turns. Base-model knowledge did not predict robustness: two models both knew 85% of the facts, yet one abandoned 25% of them and the other 80%. About 80% of the infection was in place by round 4.
- **VerifyAgent / "Epistemic Convergence Control"** (Oklahoma State, 20 Nov 2026, arXiv 2611.27839) **[read]**. Research-claim verification with Advocate, Skeptic and Arbitrator roles. A "role-collapse guard" re-runs a role when the embeddings of the two roles become too similar (cosine > 0.85). A **Belief-Delta stopping rule** halts when the belief state stabilizes. It cut over-iteration from 62.5% to 0%, used 2.4× fewer LLM calls, and anchored claims to verbatim snippets checked by an NLI model. It used one 32B model and 40 hypotheses. The hallucination improvement (25.9% → 21.7%) was not significant (p = 0.61). There was no clinical evaluation.
- Related **[titles/abstracts only]**: herding and runaway cascades (Cornell/Stanford, 2610.30769), "Phantom Consensus" (2611.08971), "Who Convinced Whom?" (2610.35930), Asch conformity in frontier LLMs (2610.36668), CMU's epistemic robustness/belief-drift study (Nov 2026), and "Surfacing Disagreement Beats Resolving It" (2611.22461).
- **So what:** the general "multi-agent debate collapses dissent" claim is now crowded. What still looks open is a **clinical (ICU/sepsis) setting with a fail-closed governance tier**. I found none of these papers using clinical data. Two ideas are worth adopting or citing: the private-probe vs. public-stance measurement from "Believed but Not Said" (a ready-made way to operationalize *dissent collapse*), and VerifyAgent's ΔB stopping rule (similar to "confidence rising without new evidence"). Read both before positioning the Score as new.

## 2. Citation detection is shifting from "does it exist" to "what is the real source" (Oct 2026)
- **CiteAnchor + CiteResolve** (UW–Madison / Waterloo / Texas A&M, arXiv 2610.30355) **[read]**. A benchmark of 423 real hallucinated citations, annotated for whether the true source can be recovered. The framework judges *citation validity* separately from *unique source recovery*. Detection F1 was 0.841 with GPT-5.6 Luna and 0.784 with an open-weight model (precision 0.875 vs. 0.751 for the CiteAudit baseline). End-to-end resolution success was 74.7%. Code and data are open (github.com/oldwizard7/CiteANCHOR_code). The paper also says **NeurIPS 2026 ran automated reference checks** after ICLR 2026, with flagged references sent to human reviewers.
- **So what:** this closely matches ARS v3.11's split between `false` and `unresolvable` and its focus on precision. CiteResolve is a ready-made external benchmark for measuring the v3.11 gate's false-positive rate, which the 2026-11-22 scan named as the selling point.

## 3. Publishers and incumbents are building it in-house (Jun–Oct 2026)
- **Springer Nature + Google DeepMind** are co-developing an AI peer-review assistant. A prototype is being tested inside the publisher's secure environment (THE, 26 Oct 2026) **[snippet]**. Separately, Nature's news team reportedly built a citation checker called **Veracity**, and Nature has worked with **Grounded AI** on flagging problematic citations **[snippet; check the primary source]**. This partly closes the open follow-up: at least one major publisher now has named tools.
- **Silverchair's ScholarOne Editor AI Console** is in pilot with 20+ publishers **[snippet]**. **Digital Science** launched the Dimensions Citation Check API (Jun 2026), which flags self-citation risk **[snippet]**. **MDPI** fully deployed its in-house integrity system, **Ethicality** **[snippet]**.
- **AAAI-26 AI review pilot** (arXiv 2604.13940): AI reviews at conference scale cost under $1 per paper, with 5,834 survey respondents **[snippet]**.

## 4. Business angle: incumbents are squeezing standalone tools
- Publisher and platform incumbents (Springer Nature/DeepMind, Silverchair, Digital Science, MDPI) are building integrity checks into submission workflows. Standalone rounds I found are small: IntegrityIQ ~$428K **[snippet]**, and sci2sci €1.2M pre-seed for quote-anchored verification of AI output in biopharma **[snippet, date unconfirmed]**. PeerTrust launched a public citation-integrity service (8 Sep 2026) **[snippet]**.
- **Read:** selling per-reference existence checks directly to authors is a commodity. The best opening is a **benchmarked, auditable component** (published false-positive rate on CiteResolve or HALLMARK, plus an audit trail) licensed to the platforms building these consoles. Domain-specific claim verification (the sci2sci pattern, which would mean clinical evidence for you) is a second option. These are signals, not market sizing.
- For reference, AI research-assistant pricing is roughly $11–89 per user per month for Elicit tiers and about $16/month for Undermind Pro. Sources conflict, so treat this as a snippet.

# Research Pulse — 2026-11-24 (scan)

This run covers news dated after the 2026-11-22 scan, plus the open follow-ups from the ledger. It does not repeat any topic or source already used.

## 1. AI peer review: in one study, AI reviews beat the human review in most pairs (5 Nov 2026)
- An Inside Higher Ed report (5 Nov) covers a study of 134 human reviews, each paired with an AI-written review. The AI review won in 100% of the pairs when another AI model judged and in 87% when human editors judged. The study has not been peer reviewed. The authors suggest that editors receive an "author dossier" that includes an AI review. The session's network blocks insidehighered.com, so this summary comes from search snippets and I could not read the article.
- At the same time, conferences are restricting AI use by reviewers. NeurIPS 2026 put hidden prompts in submitted papers to catch reviewers who use AI (as reported). ICLR 2027 allows limited AI use if the reviewer discloses it. Commentary from 3 to 9 Nov (Leiter Reports, Gelman's blog) asks whether peer review can survive unrestricted AI use by reviewers.
- **So what:** AI review quality is improving while venues tighten the rules on undisclosed use. That favors tools that make AI assistance auditable and disclosed, which is what the `academic-paper-reviewer` calibration mode and the `disclosure` mode do.
- **Business angle:** the proposed "author dossier with an AI review" would be an editor-side product. I found no evidence yet of anyone pricing it, so this is only a signal.

## 2. Citation-integrity follow-up: HalluCiteChecker is now in the ACL Anthology (AACL demos, Nov 2026)
- HalluCiteChecker now appears in the AACL 2026 demo track (ACL Anthology, Nov 2026). The arXiv preprint was already in the previous digest. A peer-reviewed open detector makes an existence-check-only offering harder to charge for.
- Georgia Tech's Mellea Legal Requirements Checker (Newswise, 2 Nov) verifies case-law citations that AI may have invented. It shows the same need outside academia. Legal is a market adjacent to the feasibility-study service.
- **Open follow-up, still unresolved:** I found no named reference-check tool run by Elsevier, Springer Nature or Wiley. One secondary blog (aiforacademic.world) says several major publishers screen references at submission but names no tool. An informal GitHub tracker logs AI-policy page changes at Elsevier and Springer Nature around 1 Nov. Both are weak evidence. Check the publishers' policy pages yourself.

## 3. Sepsis and multi-agent work: nothing new dated November
- I found nothing dated November 2026. Recent related work for your related-work list: Sepsis-Agent (SSRN, a retrospective multicohort study of 41,703 West China Hospital ICU admissions with external validation in MIMIC-IV), SepsisAgent (arXiv 2605.14723, an LLM paired with a clinical world model to choose fluids and vasopressors, 20,092 MIMIC-IV stays), and a three-agent early-sepsis system whose 10-case follow-up reported hallucinations and called for human oversight (PubMed 42167741).
- **So what:** the hallucination findings in that three-agent follow-up support the AlSamadani Score's fail-closed tiers. None of these works measures epistemic drift or dissent collapse between agents, so the niche from the last digest is still open.

## 4. Frontier and AI-for-science releases (16–21 Nov 2026)
- **NVIDIA Nemotron for Science (16 Nov, primary source):** an open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, the LitmusBench v0.2 chemistry dataset (about 50k questions), and post-training recipes for building research agents. Lila, Argonne (the StructBioReasoner multi-agent system) and KTH are early users. The headline result (61% vs 54% for GPT-6 Astra on one of Lila's tasks) is reported by NVIDIA and not independently checked.
- Aggregator sites report GPT-6.2 Astra/Sol (17 Nov), "Gemini 4 Neon", Qwen4-Max ($1.50/$4.50 per 1M tokens, 1M context) and seven Microsoft MAI models announced at Ignite. None of these is confirmed by a primary source. Agent payments also launched (ChatGPT Wallet, Replit).
- **Business angle:** open, domain-tuned "science agent" stacks are commoditizing the model layer. The parts that remain valuable are evaluation, provenance and audit, which is the ARS positioning.

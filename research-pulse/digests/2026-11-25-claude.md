# Research Pulse — 2026-11-25 (scan)

Areas covered: open follow-ups from 2026-11-22 (whether publishers run reference checks, and the funding claims), new citation-verification research, frontier agent releases in November, and pricing for AI peer review. The ledger marks citation detectors, ICU agents and sepsis trials as covered on 2026-11-22, so this scan includes only news dated after that or follow-ups.

## 1. Reference checking at publishers: Grounded AI's Veracity is the named tool (2026)
- Closes the open follow-up. Grounded AI announced partnerships covering Emerald Publishing (checks at submission for fabricated references, metadata errors and retractions), Silverchair ScholarOne (integration into editor-side integrity checks), the Beilstein-Institut, SciFlow (checks while authors write) and PublishOne. The company says eleven publishers use it. That figure is the vendor's own and has not been checked.
- Grounded AI also co-wrote a Nature analysis (Apr 2026): about 110,000 publications from 2025 (1.65%) contain at least one hallucinated citation.
- I still found no in-house tool announced by Elsevier, Springer Nature or Wiley. ScholarOne is a widely used submission platform, though, so some of their journals may reach Veracity indirectly. Unconfirmed.
- **So what:** the publisher-workflow channel that the 2026-11-22 scan identified as the likely margin now has a well-placed incumbent. It works through integrations with submission and production platforms, not direct sales to publishers. Most press coverage describes it as existence, metadata and retraction checking, not claim-to-source checking.
- **Business angle:** the evidence points to a B2B2B model, selling through the platforms (ScholarOne, SciFlow, PublishOne). A newcomer would struggle to win on existence checks. Measured false-positive rates and claim faithfulness (the ARS anchors and triangulation design) stand out more, or a newcomer could partner with or supply into Veracity-like platforms rather than compete with them.

## 2. CiteAnchor (arXiv 2610.30355, Oct 2026): checks citation validity and source recoverability separately
- New CiteResolve benchmark of 423 real hallucinated citations, each labelled for whether its original source can be recovered. Reported 0.841 F1 on detection (0.784 with an open-weight backbone), and 70.7–74.7% end-to-end resolution success, beating web-search baselines by 16–65 points. Code is on GitHub.
- **So what:** "source recoverability" means suggesting the intended real paper rather than only flagging a citation as fake. ARS v3.11 does not do this yet (it marks lookups as `false`/`unresolvable`). It could be added as an advisory "did you mean" output without changing how the gate decides. It is also a candidate benchmark for the feasibility study. Abstract only; read the paper before relying on the numbers.

## 3. Frontier agent models, November 2026: cheap, fast models for sub-agents
- The trackers list Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen (Nov 12; reported $1.50/$7.50 per 1M tokens, 1M context) and Neon (Nov 18, which can orchestrate Hydrogen sub-agents), OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol/Astra (Nov 17), Meta's Muse Ember (Nov 9, aimed at tool calls and routing) and Tencent's Hy4 (Nov 4, computer use). The tracker sources disagree with each other in places. Not checked against vendor announcements.
- **So what:** vendors are now shipping an orchestrator-plus-cheap-sub-agent tier as a product. That makes multi-agent verification pipelines, ARS included, cheaper to run. It also matters for the AlSamadani Score's drift and dissent-collapse measurements: sub-agents from the same model family will tend to agree with each other.

## 4. Pricing for AI peer review: weak evidence, with one reliable data point
- A conference AI-review pipeline reportedly processed more than 20,000 submissions at under $1 per paper (AAAI 2026, via a secondary source). Vendor examples: PeerReviewAI charges $49 per paper. Institutional licences of $5k–50k+ a year come from a content-marketing site with figures that contradict each other, so treat them as noise.
- **Business angle:** if review at a venue's scale costs under $1 a paper, reviews alone will not sustain a per-paper price. Value moves to the integrity or audit layer and to software embedded in editorial workflows, which fits finding 1.

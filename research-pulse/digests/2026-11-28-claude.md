## 1. A new benchmark shows that only enforced retrieval catches citation errors (arXiv 2611.17052, Nov 2026)
- *Auditing AI peer reviewers: a benchmark on real scientific papers* (Zubeldia, Bolliet, Villaescusa-Navarro, Villanueva-Domingo, Cranmer; Cambridge / Flatiron / UAB). The authors injected 357 errors into 30 recent preprints (15 astro-ph, 15 cs.LG), across four types (general, mathematics, numerics, citations) and three severity levels, then tested 12 reviewer systems. False-positive rates were measured on clean copies. *Read in full.*
- Key result for citations: on astrophysics, **SKEPTHICAL, the only system that must retrieve the cited PDF, detected 46.7% of citation errors. No other system exceeded 26.7%.** A Claude Code configuration had the same arXiv access, but "the execution traces show paper retrieval never took place". On ML the advantage disappears (53.3% vs 57.8–60.0%), apparently because models already know the ML literature well enough.
- Other results: on the same prompt, three frontier models differed by about 50 percentage points in total detection. Most systems had false-positive rates under 10%, and mathematics flags caused most false positives. The Stanford Agentic Reviewer scored 40.7% on astrophysics. Two ML-tuned open-weight reviewers detected almost nothing (≤2.8%). The pipeline, the injected errors and the adjudication logs are all released.
- **So what:** this is direct, measured support for the ARS design choice of a *mandatory* deterministic gate (v3.11) rather than an *optional* lookup tool. The agent skipped the optional step. It also shows that claim-to-source checking is unsolved: the best system caught under 60% of citation errors. Add this benchmark next to CiteAudit as an external test for the L3 anchor layer. It is especially useful outside ML, where model memory is weakest.

## 2. Publishers do little about fabricated references already in print: 98.4% got no action (Lancet, May 2026; still being cited in Nov)
- Topaz et al., a research letter in The Lancet (7 May 2026), screened 2.5M PMC Open Access papers published from 2023 to Feb 2026. Out of 97.1M references, 4,046 fabricated references were found in 2,810 papers. The rate rose from 1 in 2,828 papers in 2023 to 1 in 277 in early 2026. **No publisher action had been taken on 98.4% of the affected papers.** The authors urge retroactive screening. Limitations: the audit used PMID-linked references only and estimated precision but not recall. It has been criticised as "simplistic" (Hosseini).
- Policies are tightening: arXiv imposes a one-year ban and afterwards requires prior peer-reviewed acceptance. T&F usually rejects when problematic citations go beyond a small share. PLOS is "exploring system-wide reference integrity screening". Springer Nature retracted an ML book after it could not verify 25 of its 46 references.
- **Business angle:** the evidence points to an underserved **retroactive back-catalogue screening** market: publishers have the problem, The Lancet has recommended screening, and PLOS is exploring it. Today's vendors work at submission time. A low-false-positive batch audit that produces a correction-ready report is a different product. No pricing data was found, so I make no revenue claim.
- **Ledger follow-up:** Silverchair's own news pages list Cactus, Integra, Imagetwin, Signals, Clear Skies and STM Integrity Hub as ScholarOne integrity partners. **Grounded AI does not appear there.** The Silverchair–Grounded AI deal remains confirmed only by Grounded AI's own Substack.

## 3. AI peer review is becoming normal practice, and Google offers a free tool (Nov 2026)
- Inside Higher Ed (5 Nov) reports a study of 134 human reviews of 124 papers compared with AI reviews written by Claude Opus. Former journal editors preferred the AI review in **87% of pairs**. The study has not been peer reviewed.
- A Management Science paper, covered by phys.org on 19 Nov, proposes that journals run their own tuned AI reviewer before human review. It warns that this could anchor human reviewers.
- Google Deep Review gives authors **2 free reviews per week** (Academia Insider, 9 Nov).
- **Business angle:** free author-side review from Google pushes the consumer price toward zero, the same pattern seen with citation-existence checking on 11-22. The money remains on the journal side, where integration, audit trails and measured error rates (finding 1) matter. Consensus reportedly added an "Extract data" feature on 18 Nov, from a single comparison-site source. Elicit and Consensus pricing pages disagree across aggregators, and no Nov 2026 funding rounds were found.

## 4. Research by AI agents is arriving faster than the tools to verify it (Nov 2026)
- A preprint dated 19 Nov (*A Society of AI Scientists*) reports that a *Journal of Agent Research* launched on 4 Nov with a standing panel of automated model referees. Its figure of 936 agents across 35 labs is unconfirmed. ETRI (Korea) announced YouRA on 26 Nov, an end-to-end autonomous research agent that it says outperforms AI Scientist-V2 on MLR-Bench (from one news source). DiscoveryAgent (arXiv 2611.21950, 16 Nov) puts the scientist in the loop and uses audit agents to check its records.
- **So what:** papers written and refereed by agents are exactly where hallucinated citations accumulate without anyone checking them. Combined with finding 1, where agents skip optional checks, this supports positioning ARS as **verification built into the pipeline for agent-generated work**, not only for human authors.

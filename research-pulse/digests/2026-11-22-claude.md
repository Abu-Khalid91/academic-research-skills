# Research Pulse — 2026-11-22 (scan)

Areas covered: citation-integrity tooling (the feasibility-study service), multi-agent LLMs in the ICU (the AlSamadani Score), sepsis and AI, and AI-for-science funding.

## 1. Citation hallucination: several open detectors now compete, and conferences are enforcing (Jul 2026)
- A July 2026 position paper compares five detectors (CheckIfExist, HalluCiteChecker, Hallucinator, HalRef, RefChecker). Most only check that a cited paper exists. HALLMARK (Jul 2026) points out that verifiers ship without a shared benchmark. A hybrid LLM + Semantic Scholar checker misses fakes that closely resemble a real paper.
- ICLR 2026 desk-rejected papers with confirmed hallucinated references but reported a significant false-positive rate. ICML 2026 also lists hallucinated references as grounds for desk rejection. "Phantom References" (Jul 2026) documents fakes that survive peer review at top venues.
- **So what:** existence checks are becoming free and common. A service needs a different selling point, and the gaps these sources name are precision (low false positives), near-miss fakes, and claim-to-source faithfulness. That matches the ARS v3.7.3/v3.11 design: anchors, four-index triangulation, and an advisory-by-default policy.
- **Business angle:** self-serve tools sell for about $0.003–0.007 per reference (Apify actors) or $14–50/month (Citely-style subscriptions). One secondary source says institutional licenses are four figures a year and that publishers are trying mandatory reference-validation reports at submission. The low end is being driven toward zero. The likely margin is in publisher or institution workflows that come with an audit trail and a measured false-positive rate. My search found no major publisher (Elsevier, Springer Nature) running a named detection tool.

## 2. Multi-agent LLMs in the ICU: still at the feasibility stage (Jul–Aug 2026)
- PAUSE-Agents (medRxiv, Jul 2026, Northwestern): a clinician-in-the-loop multi-agent pipeline that drafts ICU-to-ward handoff briefs, with each claim traced to its source data. The authors released the pipeline and their evaluation platform.
- DeLiriuMAgents (Cell Reports Medicine-family journal on ScienceDirect, 2026): multi-agent delirium prediction. Accuracy was 0.749 on MIMIC-IV, 0.731 on PKU and 0.670 on eICU-CRD.
- Agentic ICU mortality explanations (arXiv 2608.26109, eICU demo): the agentic pipeline grounded its output in guidelines much more often (0.762 vs 0.143), but the standalone LLM matched SHAP feature importance better.
- **So what:** none of these measures whether agents become more confident without new evidence (collective epistemic drift) or whether dissent collapses during debate, which is what the AlSamadani Score targets. That still looks like an open niche. Cite PAUSE-Agents and the mortality-explanation paper as related work, and read them yourself first.

## 3. Sepsis and AI: one trial reports patient outcomes, but the AI only does quality reporting (Jun 2026)
- UCSD cluster randomized trial (JAMA Netw Open, Jun 2026): an LLM abstracted charts for SEP-1 compliance, and ED teams received feedback. Compliance improved. ICU admissions and 30-day mortality did not change. The LLM agreed with expert review 92% of the time. A commentary flags methodological limits.
- COMPOSER-LLM's journal version lists a 2026-12-01 release date. In prospective silent mode it had 72.1% sensitivity and 52.9% PPV. Chart review found that 62% of its false positives had bacterial infections.
- I found no prospective randomized trial in which an LLM makes sepsis treatment decisions and patient outcomes are reported. That is the evidence gap, and it supports the case for the AlSamadani Score's fail-closed design.

## 4. AI-for-science funding: lots of money for agents, little for autonomous research (Nov 2026)
- Nov 19 roundups list MegaRobo (~$200M, AI for Science) and Boltz among the leaders. Model ML raised $250M for finance agents. Crux Labs raised $6M+ to audit AI agents against human judgement.
- Reported, not confirmed: Cognition in talks at a $75B valuation, and Project Prometheus at $12B. Both come from single aggregator sources.
- **Business angle:** "auditing agents against human judgement" (Crux Labs) is now raising money. The AlSamadani Score's governance tiers and citation integrity are both auditing products, so the framing has investor traction. Treat this as a signal, not proof.
